The companion

It reacts. It never punishes.

Your companion reads a rolling 24-hour window of how you've cared for yourself — food, movement, sleep, water, rest — and simply looks how the day has gone. Thriving, okay, tired. That's the whole feedback loop.

A rolling 24 hours

Not a calendar day you can fail by midnight. The window moves with you, so care counts whenever you manage it.

A bar, not a number

The health bar is a segmented strip with goldenrod bonus segments for warmth from friends. No percentage is ever shown.

It speaks in observations

"Slept a little better last night." Never a nudge about what you skipped — silence is the response to absence.

Gentlest when life is hardest

Tell it you are sick and the scoring softens. A hard journal day still counts as showing up.

The friend mesh

You can only make a friend in person.

There is no friend server to hack, no follow button, no discovery feed. Two phones hold still 15 cm apart for the better part of a second, and that's the handshake. Everything after that travels device-to-device.

Three phones in one room. Nothing is connected, nothing is advertising.

1

Stand next to each other

Both of you tap Join. The phones find each other over a local, encrypted peer-to-peer link — no internet, no lobby to browse.

2

Touch phones and hold

Precise ranging measures the real distance. A friend is only committed after 15 cm for about 0.8 seconds. Phones never brought that close never connect.

3

Keys, not accounts

You exchange display names, public keys, companion looks and a fuzzy vibe. Your private keys never leave your device.

4

It fades when you leave

A session is ephemeral — hearts, recipes and hand-drawn clothing pass directly between phones, and there is nothing left on a server to delete. If you switch on the optional “deliver hearts when apart,” those hearts alone travel as sealed, unreadable ciphertext through iCloud, and only the phone that sent one can delete it.

Friends see a fuzzy vibe — thriving, okay, struggling. Never a score, never a goal, never a number, never your cycle. Your friend list stays small on purpose: 8 people.

Period tracking

The most protected thing in the app. On purpose.

Cycle tracking lives in its own sealed module. Not a setting, not a privacy mode — a wall in the source code that the AI and the networking layer cannot reach across. If someone ever wired them together by accident, the app would fail to build rather than ship.

Cycle data never leaves the phone unless you deliberately turn this on — a separate, clearly-warned opt-in, not part of ordinary iCloud sync.

Never touches the AI

On-device models cannot import the sealed store. No summaries, no suggestions, no reflections built from your cycle.

Never written to Apple Health

Fernlet reads sleep, steps and workouts from Health. It writes back only workouts — never period, mood, journal, hydration or hygiene.

Never in ordinary sync

It is excluded from iCloud sync and from your data export, so a shared laptop or a file you send someone can't carry it out with everything else.

Never visible to friends

In-person friends see a fuzzy vibe and a companion. There is no field in the handshake that could carry cycle information.

Enforced by the build, not by trust

A pre-merge script compiles the app with cross-wall imports promoted to errors. The wall is a compile-time fact, not a policy paragraph.

Intimate-activity notes sit in the same sealed store, are gated to 16+, and are hidden and off until you go looking for them.

Privacy by architecture

Not a promise in a policy. A wall in the build.

Period data, journal text, sensitive memories and intimate notes live in a sealed, encrypted store. The AI code and the networking code physically cannot import it — a build script compiles the app with cross-wall imports promoted to errors, so a violation fails the build rather than shipping.

The developer runs no server. There is nothing to breach, subpoena, or sell — because there is no copy.

Questions people actually ask

Is it free?

Yes, permanently — no price, no subscription, no in-app purchases, no ads. There is no business model that needs your data, because there is no business model that touches your data. Fernlet is also open source under Apache 2.0: every line, including the cryptography, is published at github.com/bowman-mw/fernlet, so you don’t have to take any of this on trust.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up, no login, no email, no password. The app works the moment you open it.

What happens if I miss a day?

That day scores lower. That is all. There are no streaks to break, and every unlock you have earned is cumulative and can never be taken back.

Does it count calories?

Never. You get macro goals — protein, carbs, fat — and nothing else. No calorie ring, no weight target, no body-composition anything.

Can my friends see my numbers?

No. Friends exchange display names, companion appearances and a fuzzy vibe — thriving, okay, struggling. Your score, goals, cycle data and raw health data never leave your phone.

What if I lose my phone?

If you turned on iCloud sync, your core data restores from your own iCloud. The sealed categories — cycle data, journal text, sensitive memories, intimate logs, your own photos — are a different story: their key is locked to this phone's Secure Enclave, so no device backup can bring them back on a new phone. The optional encrypted sealed backup is the only route, and it is encrypted with a key in your iCloud Keychain: if you lose access to that Keychain everywhere, that data is gone for good. That is the trade the encryption buys, and the app says so before you enable it.

Which iPhones does it need?

iOS 26 or later; iPhone 15 Pro or newer for the full on-device AI features. The 15 cm friend handshake needs a phone with precise ranging — on other phones you confirm on screen instead.
