Friends

Friends begin in person.

There is no public profile, discovery feed, or stranger requests. When someone with Fernlet is close, you'll see it on Home. Hold your phones together, 15 cm apart for the better part of a second, and you can start a hangout. Everything after that passes directly between your phones.

Three phones in one room. Nothing is connected, nothing is advertising.

1

Notice each other

When another phone with Fernlet open on Home is within about 3 m, teal rings appear: “Someone with Fernlet is close.” Until you both say yes, your phone sends only short-lived random codes, nothing that names you. It listens only while Home is on screen, never in the background or in Low Power Mode, and Connect by tapping phones in Privacy & data turns it off.

2

Hold your phones together

Ultra Wideband measures the real distance (every iOS 26 iPhone has it except iPhone SE). Only at 15 cm for about 0.8 seconds does each phone see the other's name and companion and ask “Start a hangout?” Phones never brought that close never connect.

3

No accounts involved

You exchange display names, public keys, companion looks and a fuzzy vibe. Your private keys never leave your device.

4

A hangout, then it fades

A hangout lives only while you're together: photos, messages and walks, and hearts for a friend who could use one. A heart given in person becomes a gold heart for 12 hours. Afterwards each friend's little shop opens for an hour, and at the end you choose whether to keep each other as friends. Nothing stays on a server once you go.

Friends see a name, a companion, and a broad wellbeing state — thriving, okay, struggling. They don't see your health records, score, goals, journal, cycle data or your map, and sharing ends when the connection ends. Your friends live in Album, and the list stays small on purpose: 12 friends at most, 4 of them close. One optional exception, off unless you turn it on: Deliver hearts later leaves a sealed heart for a friend in a shared iCloud drop-off, which nobody else can read.
