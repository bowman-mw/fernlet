# Accessibility Review — 2026-08-22

**Scope:** every in-app surface of the Fernlet iOS app, audited against the full iOS assistive-technology
surface (VoiceOver, Voice Control, Switch Control, Full Keyboard Access, Dynamic Type, Reduce Motion,
Increase Contrast, Differentiate Without Color, Reduce Transparency, Bold Text, Button Shapes, Braille,
Speak Screen, Assistive Access, Large Content Viewer, AssistiveTouch, Eye Tracking) plus the App Store
Accessibility Nutrition Labels obligation.

**Question this answers:** *what accessibility features can we add, and which are worthwhile?* — so every item
below carries an honest effort/impact call, and §6 and Tier 3 say plainly what **not** to build.

**Method:** 26 agents. Six feature-surface sweeps (Home/companion, Move, Food, Private/Journal, Connect,
Settings/shared UI kit) and six cross-cutting dimension sweeps (Dynamic Type, colour & contrast,
motion & haptics, motor & alternative input, VoiceOver semantics, platform features & App Store
obligations), each followed by an adversarial verifier that re-opened the cited code and re-scored impact
and effort, then a completeness critic that hunted for un-audited screens and un-covered assistive tech, then
a synthesis pass. 270 findings survived and merge to **38 work items**. Read-only throughout — no file was
edited, no build or simulator was run (a peer session held the shared DerivedData).

## How much to trust this

Three calibration facts you should weigh before funding anything:

1. **The obvious layer was already fixed six days ago, and this report is *not* a re-report of it.**
   Commit `f053be2` ("implement the 145 code-only findings from the 2026-08-16 review") is an ancestor of
   HEAD. It landed icon-only button labels, sub-44pt hit targets, `lineLimit(1)`/fixed-frame Dynamic Type
   truncation, and the light `slate` contrast bump from 3.05:1 to 4.78:1. I spot-checked three of that
   round's accessibility items: PRIV-27 (Grounding named actions) and PRIV-30 (PIN dots grouped *and*
   localized) both landed correctly; PRIV-24 (Breathing) is the one genuine residual, and it is folded into
   T0-4 below. **The implementation rate from the prior round is high** — so what is left here is the
   structural and platform layer, not basics.

2. **The verifiers were lenient about existence and strict about severity.** Of 270 findings, **zero** were
   refuted outright, but 99 were downgraded to PARTIAL and every single impact-5 rating was cut to 4 or
   below. A 0% refutation rate is a known failure signature, so I independently re-verified the report's
   load-bearing claims rather than trusting the pass. Results below.

3. **Numbers in this report were computed, not estimated.** I recomputed every WCAG ratio from the raw token
   RGB values.

## 1. Verdict

Fernlet is well above the indie baseline on everything you can *see*, and below it on everything you can only *hear*. The visual accessibility work is real and, in places, unusually disciplined: 119 `dynamicTypeSize` reads with genuine AX3/AX5 content tiering rather than stretch-testing, measured WCAG ratios written into the token doc comments (`FernletKit/Sources/FernletUI/FernletUIComponents.swift:63-97`), a shared `fernletIconButton(_:)` that bundles the 44pt target with the VoiceOver label and refuses a plain `String` (`FernletUIComponents.swift:154-179`), and Reduce Motion honoured in precisely the two surfaces that most need it — the breathing pacer (`BreathingExerciseView.swift:170`) and the worry-box tuck (`WorryBoxView.swift:387`). Zero hard-coded system colours anywhere; every colour flows through a named token, which is what makes the contrast fixes below cheap instead of a 300-site grind.

The ceiling is equally sharp and equally measurable. Across `App/` and `FernletKit/Sources/` there are **zero** uses of `.isHeader`, `.isModal`, `accessibilityCustomContent`, `accessibilityRotor`, `accessibilityInputLabels`, `@AccessibilityFocusState`, `accessibilitySortPriority`, `accessibilityIgnoresInvertColors`, `colorSchemeContrast`, `accessibilityDifferentiateWithoutColor`, `legibilityWeight`, and `keyboardShortcut` — and exactly **one** `AccessibilityNotification.Announcement` in the entire tree, inside `CaptureProtection.swift:152`. The consequence is a single coherent defect repeated ~30 times: the app is *navigable* but not *narratable*. A blind user can reach almost everything and is told almost nothing about what happened when they got there. `App/FernletWidgets/` contains zero accessibility modifiers across all 17 files.

**The single biggest risk is the app lock.** `FernletKit/Sources/FernletLockUI/FernletLockGate.swift:166` paints the unlock overlay over the Private tab without `.accessibilityHidden` or `.isModal`, so VoiceOver and Switch Control walk straight off the passcode field into an invisible-but-operable screen; `FernletLockView.swift:1103` has no `ScrollView` on an iPhone that ships with landscape enabled (`project.pbxproj:552`) and no orientation lock anywhere in code; and `FernletLockView.swift:1108` never speaks a failed attempt, the attempts-remaining warning, or `contentKeyUnrecoverable`. The mandatory gate to the most sensitive half of the app is its least accessible screen.

**The single biggest cheap win is three lines.** Adding `.accessibilityAddTraits(.isHeader)` to `SectionLabel.body` (`FernletPrimitives.swift:82`), `ScreenHeader.body` (`FernletUIComponents.swift:259`) and `SheetHeader`'s title (`SheetChrome.swift:86`) turns on the VoiceOver Headings rotor across 124 headings app-wide — 91 `SectionLabel` sites, 17 screens, 16 sheets — with no strings, no catalog churn, and no body approaching the 60-line Power-of-10 ceiling. Runner-up, also nearly free: `Color.goldenrod` inks the app's "something is misconfigured" copy at **2.22:1** on parchment, the worst contrast in the codebase, and one token pair plus ~25 mechanical swaps fixes it.

Two honest caveats before anything below is funded. Three high-impact findings rest on SwiftUI grouping semantics nobody could verify without running the app — `HomeView.swift:2381` (does `HealthBar`'s label attach to anything?), `HomeView.swift:2700-2734` (does the outer Button swallow the nested hygiene toggles?), and `WorkoutLocationSetupView.swift:699-750` (buttons inside a button) — and two of them change scope by an order of magnitude depending on the answer. Ten minutes with the Accessibility Inspector settles all three. Separately, several items land in the 25 files a peer session currently holds uncommitted; those are flagged per-item.

## 2. What already works — do not redo this

- **The 44pt/label helper pair exists and is documented.** `fernletTapTarget(minWidth:minHeight:)` (`FernletUIComponents.swift:131`) and `fernletIconButton(_: LocalizedStringKey)` (`:154`) bundle the target and the label, with a `verbatim:` twin so a literal cannot steal the localizing overload. 58 adoption sites. The gap is adoption, not design.
- **Selection state is solved at the style layer.** `ChipButtonStyle` applies `.accessibilityAddTraits(selected ? .isSelected : [])` (`FernletUIComponents.swift:866`), so every chip in the app announces correctly for free. `.isSelected` appears at 14 sites. Only six *hand-rolled* radio rows bypass it.
- **Capture protection is the best accessibility code in the repo.** (Path note, since it matters for §4.0:
  this file lives at `FernletKit/Sources/FernletUI/CaptureProtection.swift` — *inside* `FernletUI`, which is
  why the missing string catalog blocks its copy. Bare `CaptureProtection.swift` below always means that file.) `.accessibilityHidden(covered)` on covered content (`CaptureProtection.swift:411`), a single labelled cover element (`:522-524`), Reduce Motion honoured (`:471`, `:481`), and the Tier-1 nudge posted through an **injectable** announcement seam (`:152`) explicitly so a unit test can assert what was spoken. This is the template for everything in §4.
- **Dynamic Type is a design axis, not a stretch test.** Every one of the 11 type roles resolves through `.custom(_:size:relativeTo:)` (`FernletDesignSystem.swift:40-54`) — no `relativeTo:` is ever omitted. `AdaptiveStack` (`:751`), a hand-written `FlowLayout` (`:689`), `fernletWrappingText()` at 323 sites (`:422`), and `MacroRing`'s `@ScaledMetric` ring with a whole alternate AX layout (`HomeView.swift:2612-2689`) are the model implementations.
- **Contrast was measured, not eyeballed, where it was done.** `mossFill`/`onMoss`/`terracottaInk` exist because white ink measured 2.5:1 on dark fills, and light `slate` was deliberately deepened from 3.05:1 to 4.78:1 (`FernletTheme.swift:88-93`). Disabled buttons fade the *fill* and keep legible ink, with the reasoning written down (`FernletUIComponents.swift:895-899`).
- **Two gesture-only controls were already rescued properly.** The film-wind thumbwheel (`DisposableCameraView.swift:1126-1136`) has a value, a default activation and a named action, added because "VoiceOver and Switch Control users could never arm the camera." `GroundingView.swift:125-130` treats tap-anywhere as a bonus backed by named rotor actions and real buttons. These are the patterns the pixel canvas and the friend row should copy.
- **The guided workout runner never auto-advances.** Rest ends only on an explicit tap (`GuidedWorkoutRunState.swift`), and the copy says so (`GuidedWorkout.swift:393`). Most workout apps fail this outright; here it falls out of the state machine.
- **`ExerciseHistoryView.swift`** and **`QuickLogShortcutsEditor.swift:42-47`** are clean. The latter uses `List` + `.onMove`, which SwiftUI exposes natively as VoiceOver move actions — it is the in-repo fix for the hand-rolled Home-widget reorder two files away.
- **Correctly N/A, verified:** no `import Charts`, no `AVPlayer`/`AVKit` anywhere, `TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY = 1`. No chart accessibility work, no captions, no audio descriptions, no iPad size-class work is owed.

**T0-1.** `FernletLockGate.body(content:)` is a `ZStack { content; if isLocked { lockOverlay.zIndex(100) } }` — `zIndex` reorders drawing, not the accessibility tree. A VoiceOver user swiping right past the biometric button lands in the Private hub's headers, the worry composer and the cycle grid, and can activate them, while the lock is painted over the screen. Add `.accessibilityHidden(active && (isLocked || isNotConfigured))` to `content` and `.accessibilityAddTraits(.isModal)` to both overlays — the sibling module already does exactly this at `CaptureProtection.swift:411`. Sealed bodies render as "This entry is sealed" while locked, so the *leak* half is unproven; the *navigation* half is an unambiguous break of the one screen that must be modal. No strings, no wall interaction, `body` is ~20 lines.

**T0-2.** The unlock body is a bare `VStack` of header + error + attempts + dots + a 4×(76×56pt) keypad ≈ 272pt of pad alone. `INFOPLIST_KEY_UISupportedInterfaceOrientations_iPhone` declares landscape and zero code locks orientation. In landscape the keypad clips below the fold with no way to scroll to it. Wrap in `ScrollView` + `.scrollBounceBehavior(.basedOnSize)`; the body is already near the 60-line Power-of-10 ceiling, so extract `unlockStack` first and say so in the commit. While there, the digits are `.fernlet(.stat)` — 14pt, half of iOS's own passcode keypad — inside a *hard* frame, so raise to `.custom(dmSansMedium, size: 24, relativeTo: .title2)` and change `.frame(width:height:)` to `minWidth:/minHeight:`.

**T0-3.** Every distinguishing lock-screen state is silent: the error `Text` at `:1108`, the attempts warning at `:1119`, the bare `ProgressView()` at `:1233`, and the unrecoverable-key state at `:1436`. Post announcements from `attemptUnlock()`'s catch arms, bind `@AccessibilityFocusState` to the error `Text`, label the spinner, and add `.sensoryFeedback(.error, trigger: errorMessage)`. **Localization wall:** this file is inside the `FernletLockUI` SPM module — reuse the existing `FernletLockCopy` strings, which already resolve with `bundle: .module`; a fresh literal here renders English forever with a clean build. **Security:** add the announcement only to the *failure* arms, never keyed off which credential matched, or you build a duress oracle. The pad's phantom disabled key (`:1614`) and unlabeled backspace ride along here.

**T0-4.** The session loop sets `phaseLabel`, fires an identical `.soft` haptic for inhale/hold/exhale, animates, sleeps — no announcement, no focus, and both circles are `.accessibilityHidden(true)`. With haptics off (the toggle at `:400` is a `Toggle("", isOn:)` with an *empty* label, so VoiceOver announces "switch button, on" with no name and Voice Control cannot target it) the exercise conveys nothing at all for up to three minutes. Cheapest correct fix: make `runningScreen` one element with `.accessibilityElement(children: .ignore)` + a stable label + `.accessibilityValue(phaseLabel)` — a changed *value* on the focused element is re-spoken; a changed `Text` is not. Fix the toggle in the same commit (`Toggle("Haptics", isOn:).labelsHidden()`). **The contrast half of this item is already resolved — in flight.** `BreathingExerciseView.swift` inked the `End early` button — the only exit from a 1–3 minute session — in `Color.softTaupe`, which I measured at **2.03:1**, while every other muted label in that same file already used `Color.slate` (4.78:1). It was a straight miss by the 2026-08-16 round, whose PRIV-24 called it out. On being shown the measurement the peer session applied `Color.slate` (now line 222, with the reasoning in a comment at 217-219); **verified on disk, riding their capstone commit.** Nothing left to do here. The rest of T0-4 — the missing pacing — stands: this file still contains **zero** announcements. `BreathPhase.label` is a plain `String` literal today, so fork it to a catalog key or the announcement ships English.

**T0-5.** `keepItWindow = .seconds(6)` gates permanent deletion of a sealed row deliberately absent from `SealedBackup`. A VoiceOver user must realise a new bottom overlay exists, swipe to it, and double-tap inside 6 s; a Switch Control scan cannot complete one pass. Mail's Undo Send — cited in the screen's own doc comment as the model — is 10–30 s and user-configurable. Fix: announce on strip appearance, bind `@AccessibilityFocusState` to "Keep it", and compute the window as 20 s when `isVoiceOverRunning || isSwitchControlRunning`. **Do not** apply `.isModal` to the toast as one auditor proposed — it would hide the entire page for the full 6 s. Keep `commitPendingRelease()` on `.onDisappear` exactly as it is.

**T0-6.** `goNext()` is one line: `withAnimation { store.cookingAdvanceStep() }`. `.id(stepIndex)` gives the step text a fresh identity, tearing down the focused element without moving the cursor. A wet-handed blind cook must swipe backwards through the footer, progress row and timer card after every tap. Add `@AccessibilityFocusState` bound to the step text, `.accessibilityAddTraits(.isHeader)`, and a position-bearing label `Text("Step \(n) of \(m). \(step.text)")` — but drive the focus from `.onChange(of: stepIndex)`, **not** from inside `goNext()`, where the new view does not exist yet. ~5 lines. Add `Announcement("Timer's up")` beside `fireHaptic()` (`:749`) at the same time; do **not** put `.accessibilityLabel` on the `Text(timerInterval:)` — it replaces the live countdown.

**T0-7.** `LogPeriodSheet.save()` sets `statusMessage` on the dropped-narrative path, sleeps 1.5 s, and dismisses. That message tells the user their sealed note was *not kept*. It is rendered as a `Text`, VoiceOver focus is elsewhere for the whole window, and a blind user is structurally guaranteed never to hear it. `LogIntimacySheet`'s hidden-tracking path does not even dismiss — the sheet stays open with the entry unwritten and nothing spoken. This contradicts the project's own nothing-silent invariant, which currently holds visually and fails for assistive tech. Prefer *not auto-dismissing on a non-clean save* over lengthening the timer. **Privacy:** announce only the outcome sentence, never the narrative text — an announcement is audible.

## 4. Systemic moves

These five are architectural rather than per-screen. Three of them are prerequisites: schedule them ahead of the Tier 2 items they unblock.

**4.0 (prerequisite) — `FernletUI` has no string catalog, and that silently blocks six proposed fixes.** `Localizable.xcstrings` exists in `FernletDomainModel`, `FernletLockUI`, the app and both extensions — and **not** in `FernletUI`, whose `Package.swift` target declares no `resources:`. This is by design (the module takes `LocalizedStringKey`/`Text` from callers and owns no copy), but it means any accessibility literal added *inside* `FernletUI` resolves against `Bundle.main` and renders English forever with a clean build. It blocks T2-16 (`SheetField`/`SheetTextEditor` labels), the `SheetSaveBar` disabled reason, `CaptureProtection`'s cover text (`:501`) and nudge copy (`:301`), and `EmptyState`. Two literals already violate it today (`FernletUIComponents.swift:484`, `CaptureProtection.swift:500-502`). **Cost:** add a catalog and wire `resources:` (S), *or* route every one of these through caller-supplied parameters (also S, and arguably cleaner). **Breaks:** nothing, if done first. Discovering it later means six fixes that ship and appear to work.

**4.1 — A shared accessible-row contract in `FernletUI`.** The app has ~208 rows built from `SectionLabel` (91) and `SheetField` (117), and no vocabulary for "name goes in the label, changing state goes in the value, detail goes on the rotor." Introduce a small set of modifiers — `fernletRowSemantics(label:value:)`, a `SheetField(label:content:)` variant that pairs its caption to a *single* control, and a documented custom-content convention — and adopt them at the four dense list surfaces (`MealRow`, `peerRow`, the AI audit log, the friend-photo grid). **Buys:** T2-2, T2-4, T2-16 collapse into call-site adoption instead of per-screen invention. **Costs:** M, and it must be a single-control *opt-in* — a blanket `.accessibilityLabeledPair(role: .content,)` across 117 heterogeneous `@ViewBuilder` contents would stamp the caption onto every chip in `WorkoutSetupView.swift:250-284`. **Breaks:** element grouping at ~117 call sites, several of which UI tests query by label — the `hubLink` precedent (`SettingsSheet.swift:326-332`) exists precisely because folding a sub-label into the label broke `buttons["…"]` lookups. Sweep the UI suites in the same commit.

**4.2 — A contrast capability in the token layer.** Today `FernletThemePalette.current(for:)` takes one axis (`UIUserInterfaceStyle`) and the four dynamic providers throw away `trait.accessibilityContrast` they already hold. Widen it to `current(for:contrast:)`, add the `*Ink` text siblings from T1-3, add `@Environment(\.colorSchemeContrast)` branches to `FernletCard` (91 refs), `ChipButtonStyle` (45) and `ActionPillButtonStyle` (37), and introduce one `@Environment(\.accessibilityDifferentiateWithoutColor)` read for the glyph-vs-hue cases. **The palette is already designed and approved — this is implementation, not design.** The same spec specifies the Increase Contrast twins outright: *"AX twins run Increase Contrast: slate → #45535E, card edges 30%, filled moss → #38562C"*. I verified both: `#45535E` measures 6.90:1 / 7.41:1 and `#38562C` measures 7.22:1 / 7.75:1 (white on it, 8.28:1). Neither hex, nor `colorSchemeContrast`, appears anywhere in the code — the peer session implemented the AX twins' *layout* degradations but the *contrast* half had nowhere to land. Cleanest shape: extend the existing `Color(light:dark:)` helper (`FernletUIComponents.swift:103`) to `Color(light:dark:lightHC:darkHC:)` resolving inside the **same** `UIColor` dynamic provider, which already receives `trait.accessibilityContrast` — one helper change, zero call-site churn. **Buys:** the Increase Contrast and Sufficient Contrast ANL rows become claimable; component boundaries currently sitting at 1.15–1.73:1 clear the 3:1 non-text floor. **Costs:** M for the seam, and note two traps — `bark.opacity(0.35)` measures **1.99:1**, not the 3.1:1 one auditor asserted (you need ~0.55 alpha), and a `ButtonStyle` cannot read the environment directly, so `ChipButtonStyle` needs the nested-`Pill` shape `ActionPillButtonStyle` already uses at `:912`. **Breaks:** nothing at default settings — every branch is behind `.increased`. Separately, the ~30 `.foregroundStyle(Color.slate.opacity(0.4–0.7))` sites cannot be fixed with a "muted but ≥4.5:1" token: slate at *full* strength is only 4.78:1 on parchment, so muted ink and AA are mutually exclusive in this palette. Differentiate by size and weight instead, and say so.

**4.3 — A focus and modality convention for sheets and overlays.** Zero `@AccessibilityFocusState`, zero `.isModal`, zero `.accessibilitySortPriority` across 86 sheets, 51 alerts and 5 blocking overlays. The convention should be three rules, not 86 retrofits: (a) `.accessibilitySortPriority(10)` on `SheetHeader`'s title so the sheet's name is read before "Cancel" — one shared line, all 16 sites; (b) `.isModal` + `.accessibilityHidden` on anything that covers content; (c) move focus *only* where content the user was reading is destroyed or replaced — which is a per-sheet opt-in (`focusesTitleOnAppear: Bool = false`), never a blanket default, because forcing title focus would fight the text field on the delete-everything gate. **Buys:** the ANL VoiceOver criterion "cursor moves logically when screens change or modals appear," which is currently a named blocker. **Costs:** M. **Breaks:** SwiftUI sheet focus timing is genuinely unreliable; every focus site needs a device check, and the 8 existing `@FocusState` sites are all candidates for the same "keyboard focus moved, VoiceOver cursor orphaned" defect and want one sweep.

**4.4 — An announcement and haptic vocabulary.** Extract `FernletAnnouncer` from the `CaptureProtection.swift:152` seam (T1-2) and define a small closed set: `.success` after a durable write, `.error` in every catch arm that currently only assigns a message (`ConnectView.swift:895`, `:175`, `DisposableCameraView.swift:1337`, `FernletLockView.swift:1440`), `.increase` on counter taps, and an announcement on every state message that is currently rendered as a silent `Text` (`setOperationError` at `PrivacyDataSettingsView.swift:517` is the single cleanest seam in the app — one function, already carrying a resolved `String`, zero new catalog entries). **Buys:** ~30 findings become one-line call sites, *and* become unit-testable through the injected closure. **Costs:** M. **Breaks:** nothing at runtime, but the localization trap is severe — `Announcement` takes a `String`, so the API must be `LocalizedStringResource` at its surface. **Never** announce sealed content; announce the event.

**4.5 — A mechanical accessibility wall, in this repo's own idiom.** Three separate agents independently proposed three walls (`DynamicTypeBoundaryTests`, `ColorContrastBoundaryTests`, `AccessibilitySemanticsBoundaryTests`). **Build one.** They share a scanner and an allowlist, and the repo's precedent — `Scripts/power-of-10-scan.py` + a Swift-port boundary test + one allowlist JSON where each entry states the invariant that makes it safe — is exactly the shape. Two halves:

- **The grep half** (`Scripts/accessibility-scan.py` + `Tests/FernletTests/AccessibilityBoundaryTests`). Ship only the rules that are actually greppable: **R1** no `.accessibilityElement(children: .combine)` immediately followed by `.accessibilityLabel` (allowlist the 5 deliberate sites — `HomeView.swift:2670`, `:1744`, `FoodCaptureRouter.swift:209`, `FernletUIComponents.swift:1517`, and one more — where the replacement label says strictly *more* than the fragments); **R2** no `.rawValue` inside any accessibility-modifier argument; **R3** `SectionLabel`/`ScreenHeader`/`SheetHeader` must each contain `.isHeader` (a regression canary for T1-1); **R4** `Font.custom` without `relativeTo:` (currently zero — start the baseline clean); **R5** `GridItem(.adaptive(minimum: <literal>))` requires a `@ScaledMetric`. **Do not** ship the tempting "an accessibility label may not be a bare `String`" rule as a grep — it cannot distinguish `.accessibilityLabel(coverText)` (a `String`, a real defect) from `.accessibilityLabel(titleKey)` or `.accessibilityLabel(accessibilityLabelText)` (a `Text`, correct). That rule belongs in **`LocalizationBoundaryTests`** as a *type* check on the ~18 named `String`-returning members, and it is the highest-value single rule available. Do not duplicate it in both suites; pick one home.
- **The runtime half.** `XCUIApplication.performAccessibilityAudit(for:_:)` bolted onto the existing 31-screen `Tests/FernletUITests/UXScreenProbe.swift` harness, which already launches with `-completeOnboarding`, a demo seed and a sheet-opening hook. Chain `.audit()` into `capture()`. **One correction to every version of this proposal:** `performAccessibilityAudit` **throws**, so the probe method must be `throws` (every one of the 31 chained call sites gains `try`) — `try!` and swallowed `try?` are both banned, and `XCTAssertNoThrow` discards the issue handler. Start with `.all.subtracting(.contrast)` if the parchment palette is noisy, and burn the contrast allowlist down deliberately. **Buys:** independent confirmation of the Larger Text and Sufficient Contrast ANL rows, and a floor under everything in Tiers 0–2. **Costs:** M. **Honest ceiling to document:** it catches unlabeled elements, sub-44pt hit regions, clipped text and low-contrast pairs. It does **not** catch focus order, wrong-but-present labels, missing custom actions, or Reduce Motion. Those stay manual, forever.

## 5. App Store Accessibility Nutrition Labels

Apple's Accessibility Nutrition Labels render a section on the product page and make the **developer** responsible for verifying that *all common tasks* work with each declared feature ([App Store Connect › Manage app accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/), [Larger Text evaluation criteria](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/larger-text-evaluation-criteria)). It is a form, not an API — so for this repo the deliverable is `Docs/Accessibility-Nutrition-Label.md`, written in the style of `Docs/No-Tracking-Wall.md`: one row per feature, the Apple criterion, the current verdict, and the named blocker for every "no." An over-claim is worse for a disabled user than a blank, and Fernlet has never been submitted, so it gets the rare luxury of deciding its declarations *before* the first submission rather than retrofitting them.

| Row | Today | Blocker | Earliest honest declaration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dark Interface** | **Claimable now** | none — full adaptive palette via UIColor dynamic providers (`FernletUIComponents.swift:19-70`), measured ratios documented (`:66-71`) | **Declare at first submission** |
| **Captions** | **N/A** | zero `AVFoundation`/`AVKit`/`AVPlayer` in the tree — no timed media exists | Leave blank (not "not supported") |
| **Audio Descriptions** | **N/A** | same | Leave blank |
| **Reduced Motion** | No | 6 ungated `TimelineView(.animation)` + 1 `repeatForever`, including the always-on companion breath | **After T1-6** — one batch |
| **Differentiate Without Color** | No | zero `accessibilityDifferentiateWithoutColor` reads; `WorkoutSplit.color` collides (`.workout`/`.cardio` both terracotta, `.upper`/`.push`/`.pull` all moss) and `MoveView.swift:3266-3275` encodes calendar-cell category by hue alone with logged-vs-planned at `.opacity(0.38)` on a 5pt circle | **After T1-5 + the calendar-dot glyph fix** — near-term, and cheaper than it looks because nearly every other dot already sits beside its label |
| **Sufficient Contrast** | Unverified | light-mode accents fail as text (goldenrod 2.22:1); ~30 `.opacity()` foreground sites at 1.70–2.74:1; no `colorSchemeContrast` branch anywhere | **After T1-3 + §4.2 + the contrast half of the audit wall** |
| **Larger Text** | Close, unverified | Apple requires verified no-truncation at AX5 across all common tasks. 13 sites *delete* content at AX sizes; fixed-pixel grid minimums truncate equipment and care-task labels | **After T2-2 + T2-11 + a `.dynamicType` audit pass on all 31 probe screens** |
| **Voice Control** | Unverified | zero `accessibilityInputLabels`; several controls' names are full sentences; `onTapGesture`-instead-of-`Button` rows have no name to speak | **After T2-12 + T2-3** |
| **VoiceOver** | No | criterion is *complete all common tasks using only VoiceOver*. Blocked today by Tier 0 (the lock screen), by the widget target's zero labels, and by "cursor moves logically when screens change" (§4.3) | **After Tiers 0–2 plus a manual task-by-task pass.** Do not declare it early |

**Recommended first-submission set: Dark Interface only, or Dark Interface + Reduced Motion + Differentiate Without Color if Tier 1 ships first.** Leave Captions and Audio Descriptions blank as genuinely inapplicable. Leave VoiceOver, Voice Control, Larger Text and Sufficient Contrast blank rather than over-claiming; they can be added at any subsequent submission, and a blank row costs a user nothing while a wrong row costs them a wasted download and a broken task. One platform note worth putting in the doc: Apple explicitly forbids citing Hover Text as evidence of Larger Text support. And because the no-tracking wall means no declaration can ever be validated by field telemetry, the wall here has to be a doc **plus** the test in §4.5 — that is the whole point of building it.

## 6. Explicitly out of scope / not worth it

- **The pixel-art Creation Studio cannot be made meaningfully usable with VoiceOver at acceptable cost.** Pixel art is irreducibly visual; the editor grids run up to 48×40 (`CustomItemModels.swift:48-65`), so a per-cell rotor is ~1,920 swipe stops, and a full cursor mode is a day-plus plus its own view model. **Mitigation:** ship Tier 1 of the canvas (`allowsDirectInteraction` so VoiceOver stops intercepting touches, a describing `accessibilityValue`, zoom custom actions) so Voice Control and Switch Control users can at least paint freehand and low-vision users can navigate the tool — then spend the *rest* of that budget making the friend shop and a set of starter items fully accessible, so a blind user can dress their companion without ever drawing. The reward loop is degraded, not severed.
- **Bold Text is not a "we forgot to check the setting" problem; it is a typeface problem.** The two Instrument Serif files in `App/Fernlet/Fonts/` *are* the entire family, and that face carries body copy and every section header. There is no heavier cut to bundle. Say so in the ANL doc rather than leaving the row looking like an oversight.
- **Speak Screen needs no work of its own** — it reads the same tree VoiceOver does, and Fernlet's long-form content is all real `Text`. It is the *reason* T1-8 got promoted from polish, and nothing more.
- **Braille needs no separate workstream.** A braille display mirrors the VoiceOver string, so every label item is already a braille item. The only genuine divergences are `FingerprintText` (hex reads fine on braille, badly in speech — T2-20) and `"3 / 8"` progress strings (literal on braille, "3 slash 8" in speech).
- **Eye Tracking, Dwell, AssistiveTouch and Guided Access need no dedicated items.** Eye Tracking's entire requirement is ≥44pt targets and no hover-dependent UI, which is exactly T1-9. AssistiveTouch partially rescues the camera-wind and canvas gestures but not for a user who cannot make the gesture once to record it — add it to those items' AT lists and move on. Guided Access has no code surface here.
- **iPad, size classes, and Sound Recognition / Music Haptics / Live Captions / Hover Text are all genuinely N/A.** `TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY = 1`; the rest are system-level features with no third-party API or no surface in this app. Recording these prevents a future audit from re-discovering them as gaps.
- **Six of the 270 inventory entries are scoping notes, not findings** — each is `polish/impact 1` and concludes "don't do this." Keep the reasoning as an appendix; the honest defect count is ~262. This is good work mis-filed, and the negative findings are among the most useful things in the audit.

## 7. Suggested sequencing

Sized like this repo's existing rounds: one branch per batch, commit before starting the next (the shared-worktree hazard is documented in project memory), and run tests in batches rather than as one suite.

**Batch 1 — "The lock, the calm tools, and the destructive clock" (Tier 0; ~3–4 days).**
Contains T0-1 through T0-7. Two files dominate: `FernletLockUI/FernletLockView.swift` + `FernletLockGate.swift`, and the three therapeutic surfaces. **Verify:** manual VoiceOver pass on unlock (wrong PIN → is the failure spoken? does focus survive?), rotate the device to landscape on the unlock screen at default *and* AX5, a VoiceOver run of one full breathing session and one cooking walk with the screen off-attention, and a stopwatch on the worry-box release with VoiceOver running. Then the existing lock suite plus `PrivateDataUITests`. **Nothing here needs a design decision** — that is deliberate, so this batch can land while the peer session's redesign is still uncommitted.

**Batch 2 — "Shared components and tokens" (T1-1, T1-3, T1-4, T1-5, T1-6, T1-8, T1-9; ~1 week).**
The whole batch is component and token edits. **Depends on:** the peer session committing the 25 in-flight files — `FernletUIComponents.swift`, `SheetChrome.swift` and `SettingsSheet.swift` all appear here. **Needs one design decision before starting:** the chip-density change in T1-9. **Verify:** re-derive every contrast ratio from the new literals (do not trust the numbers in the audit — two of the auditors' proposed hex values were wrong by a factor of 1.5); toggle Reduce Motion in the simulator and confirm the companion, launch screen, ambience and scanner all still; VoiceOver rotor → Headings on Settings, Food and the recipe detail; and a UI-test sweep for label-based queries broken by the `.isSelected` and glyph-hiding changes. This is also where the **audit wall's runtime half** should land (§4.5), because it gates everything after it.

**Batch 3 — "The announcer, focus, and the widget target" (T1-2, T1-7, T1-10, T2-3, T2-5, T2-13, T2-15; ~1 week).**
Depends on Batch 2 only for merge order. `FernletAnnouncer` lands first with unit tests on the injected closure (copy `CaptureProtection`'s existing test shape), then the ~12 adoption sites, then the focus convention, then the widget/Live Activity labels. **Verify:** unit tests asserting exactly which announcements fire and which do not (this is the whole reason to extract the seam); a VoiceOver pass on meal logging end-to-end including Undo; add the widget and both accessory families to a Home Screen and Lock Screen with VoiceOver on. **Watch:** the sheet-focus work needs a device, not the simulator, and any focus behaviour you cannot reproduce twice should be dropped rather than shipped.

**Batch 4 — "The localization wall's accessibility half" (§4.0, T2-1, T2-8, T2-16, T2-19; ~1 week).**
Start with §4.0 (`FernletUI` catalog or caller-supplied params) — six fixes are silently blocked on it. Then `DestructiveConfirmation`'s `String` → `LocalizedStringKey` fork (one type, 23 dialogs), the ~18 `String`-returning accessibility members, the steppers and numeric fields, and `NotificationService`'s two hardcoded bodies. **Verify:** the new `LocalizationBoundaryTests` rule green at zero, `Scripts/sync-string-catalogs.sh --check` clean, and a pseudo-locale run to confirm the forked strings actually resolve. **Note for the AX3/AX5 contract doc:** every `isAccessibilitySize` branch and `minimumScaleFactor` in the tree is currently calibrated against English string lengths; German runs 30–40% longer and will re-break them. One sentence now, expensive to discover later.

**Batch 5 — "Per-surface debt and the declaration" (remaining Tier 2 + the ANL doc; ~1–2 weeks).**
T2-2, T2-4, T2-6, T2-7, T2-9, T2-10, T2-11, T2-12, T2-14, T2-17, T2-18, T2-20, T2-21, T2-22 — largely independent of one another, so this batch can be split or parallelised. Finish by writing `Docs/Accessibility-Nutrition-Label.md` and running the manual per-feature task passes it commits you to. **Verify:** the audit wall's `.contrast` and `.dynamicType` types enabled across all 31 probe screens with the allowlist burned down; a Smart Invert pass over the photo and QR surfaces; a cold launch in dark mode to confirm the flash is gone; and one end-to-end VoiceOver-only session doing the app's four common tasks (log a meal, log water, log a mood, complete a workout) as the gate for whether the VoiceOver row can ever be declared.
